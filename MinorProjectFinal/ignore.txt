NRZ-L was selected as the VLC line-coding scheme because of its simplicity and low implementation overhead. Each binary data bit is represented by a constant optical HIGH or LOW level for one fixed bit period, allowing the ESP32 transmitter to perform encoding directly through GPIO timing and the receiver to recover the bits through ADC thresholding. This provides an uncomplicated baseline for evaluating the VLC channel and calculating BER before introducing more complex line-coding or error-control techniques.



Manchester encoding was initially investigated but was found to be more sensitive to timing synchronization in the prototype, particularly due to the use of different ESP32 boards at the transmitter and receiver. Since Manchester represents each data bit using two signal intervals, accurate half-bit timing is required for reliable decoding. NRZ-L was therefore selected as the baseline line-coding scheme because each bit is represented by a single fixed-duration optical level, reducing timing and decoding complexity while providing sufficient performance for the controlled VLC experiments.
